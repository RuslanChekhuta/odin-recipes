Lasagna

Description

Lasagna is a classic Italian dish made of stacked layers of flat pasta alternated with various fillings and baked in an oven. Originating in Italy, this traditional comfort food has become popular worldwide, often served in large casseroles as a hearty family meal. The name itself refers to both the wide, rectangular sheets of pasta and the final culinary creation, which embodies the warmth and richness of authentic Italian cooking.

The flavor of lasagna is deeply savory, comforting, and complex, blending the tanginess of tomato sauce with the rich creaminess of cheese and meat. Standard ingredients include wide lasagna noodles, a robust ragù (ground meat cooked with tomatoes, onions, and herbs), and a luscious cheese mixture typically featuring ricotta, mozzarella, and Parmesan—or a traditional European Béchamel sauce. Baked until bubbling and golden-brown on top, the magic of lasagna lies in how the edges turn delightfully crispy while the center remains incredibly soft, melted, and flavorful.
